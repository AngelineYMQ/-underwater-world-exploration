Learning to Dive

When theory finally met the ocean.

Diving was difficult at first. That difficulty became part of what made the experience meaningful.

1 · Theory

Safety first

I began by learning equipment, safety protocols and written theory. At first, much of it felt abstract.

2 · Pool

Learning the skills

In the pool I had to adjust to breathing through my mouth, checking equipment and remembering underwater communication gestures.

3 · Sea

The real environment

Once I entered the sea, I understood why both theory and practical skill mattered. But something else captured my attention: rubbish in the water.

What I expected to remember was safety.

Instead, I remembered plastic bags and bottles.

As I went deeper, I saw less rubbish, but it still appeared from time to time. When I came out of the water, I kept thinking about what I could do about it.

What diving taught me beyond diving

Theory needs practice

Knowing the names of equipment is different from checking and using it correctly. Knowledge becomes useful when I can apply it.

Communication matters

Underwater, clear hand signals are not optional. They showed me how important it is to understand a shared system before entering a challenging environment.
