My Journey

One dive changed what I noticed.

Before this website, there was a chain of small experiences that kept pulling me back to the ocean.

One year before

Picking up trash on the beach

I had already started noticing litter along the coast and picking it up. It was a simple action, but it made me pay more attention to what reaches the sea.

June 2024

Learning to dive

Living in a country surrounded by sea made me want to understand the underwater world. I began in the pool, learning how to breathe, communicate and remember safety procedures.

First sea dive

Seeing pollution below the surface

When I finally entered the sea, I expected to worry most about diving safety. Instead, I saw plastic bags, bottles and rubbish. I came out thinking about how I could help.

13 June 2024

Sharing at PingShan School

With my friend Tom, I spoke to students about ocean animals and ocean pollution. Their curiosity stayed with me and showed me that sharing what I learn can matter too.

PYP Exhibition

Exploring microplastics

For my Grade 6 PYP Exhibition, I continued the same theme through research on microplastics, marine animals and the food chain. I created whale models and a handmade puzzle to make the issue visible.

Now

Turning the journey into a website

This website is the next step: a place to help other young people learn about marine life around us, explore responsibly and find small ways to protect the ocean.

Marine Life Around Singapore

You do not have to travel far to discover marine life.

Singapore's shores include sandy beaches, rocky shores, seagrass meadows and other coastal habitats. Here are a few animals young explorers may learn about.

⭐
Sea star

Knobbly Sea Star

A striking sea star recorded in Singapore's intertidal areas. Observe it where it lives instead of lifting or moving it.

🐙
Cephalopod

Octopus

Octopuses can be found in intertidal habitats. Their camouflage can make them difficult to spot, so careful observation matters.

🦀
Crustacean

Hermit Crab

Hermit crabs rely on empty shells as homes. This is one reason even empty-looking seashells should be left on the shore.

🥒
Sea cucumber

Sea Cucumber

Different sea cucumbers live in Singapore's coastal habitats. They may look harmless, but wildlife is best observed without handling.

🐡
Fish

Pufferfish

Pufferfish are among the animals documented in Singapore's intertidal monitoring. Give wildlife space and never try to provoke a reaction.

🪸
Habitat builder

Hard Coral

Hard corals occur along Singapore's coasts. They are living animals and part of a fragile habitat, not souvenirs.

Know Before You Touch

The safest rule: observe first.

Some marine animals can bite, pinch or sting, and many fragile animals can also be harmed by handling. Responsible exploration protects both you and the wildlife.

Do

Wear covered shoes. Watch every step. Photograph wildlife where you find it. Learn with a trained guide when possible. Take your litter home.

Do not

Do not touch unfamiliar animals, collect wildlife, remove shells, poke animals with tools, or step into murky areas you cannot see clearly.

Important: This website is for learning and does not replace guidance from trained professionals. If you are unsure what an animal is, do not touch it.
Take Action

Small actions become stronger when they continue.

You do not need to be a scientist to begin. Start with what you can see, learn, share and change.

Notice

Look closely at your local shore. What animals, litter or changes do you see?

Learn

Use trustworthy resources to identify wildlife and understand the habitat.

Share

Teach a friend, classmate or younger child one thing you discovered.

Act

Reduce single-use plastic, join a clean-up or contribute to citizen science.

Why I Made This

Curiosity is where action starts.

I first thought diving would teach me about the underwater world. It did — but not in the way I expected. Seeing rubbish in the sea made the problem feel real. Speaking to other students showed me that learning can spread. My PYP Exhibition helped me explore the issue more deeply.

This website brings those experiences together. I hope it helps another young person look at the shore differently, ask one more question, and choose one action that protects the world below the water.
